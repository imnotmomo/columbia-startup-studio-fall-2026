# Our top two ideas

These come from the two highest totals in the [dot vote](dot-vote-result.md): #6 with 7 votes and #12 with 3. Each builds on its [flip](flips.md) rather than the original bad idea.

## 1. Pola: indoor navigation built on TopoNavi

Map apps get you to the building and then stop. Pola gives directions from the building's entrance to the exact room: which floor, which stairs or elevator, and which way to turn in the hallway. It runs on TopoNavi, an indoor routing engine that describes a building's floors, hallways, stairs, elevators, and access rules (such as doors that need a card), so it doesn't rely on GPS inside.

**The problem:** inside a building, GPS is unreliable and maps don't know rooms or floors. People fall back on signs, room numbers, asking someone, or walking until they find the room. Those take time and sometimes send them the wrong way.

**Who has it:** students in their first weeks, with short gaps between classes in buildings they haven't been inside. Also visitors with somewhere to be on time, such as an interview or an appointment.

**Why they'd care:** a wrong hallway can turn a 10-minute gap into a late arrival, and walking in late or asking for directions is embarrassing. For a visitor, being late can cost the meeting itself.

**Where it came from:** #6, the Unstoppable Class-Punctuality Wearable. The constant tracking and the vibration you can't turn off were the bad parts. The useful part was the engine underneath, which knows the route inside the building. It also answers the flip of #2: GPS already gives wrong directions indoors. See the TopoNavi sections of [lean canvas 6](lean-canvas/6.md).

## 2. Photo reconnection

The app suggests one photo from albums the user has approved, plus two or three people who were there. The user chooses whether to send it and to whom. Nothing is sent automatically.

**The problem:** students meet many people through classes, clubs, and dorms but lose touch, because messaging someone without a reason feels awkward. Meanwhile, photos of moments they shared sit unseen in the camera roll.

**Who has it:** college students with many acquaintances who aren't close friends, especially first-years, club members, and anyone who recently met a lot of new people.

**Why they'd care:** those acquaintances are future friends, study partners, and contacts, and they slip away quietly. A shared photo makes the first message easy and gives the other person something to reply to.

**Where it came from:** #12, the Random Camera-Roll Snapchat. It keeps the surprise and the reason to reach out, and drops the random photos and random recipients that made it unsafe. See [lean canvas 12](lean-canvas/12.md).
