# Flips for the worst business ideas

For each bad idea in the [brainstorm](Worst_Business_Ideas_Brainstorm.docx), the flip asks what is interesting or good inside it. Numbers and names follow the brainstorm and the [dot vote](dot-vote-result.md). Ideas 7 and 9 were left off the dot vote but are flipped here too. Flips that led to our [top two ideas](top-two-ideas.md) are marked.

## 1. ADHD "Smart Tray"

**Bad idea:** A tray that checks whether you took your medication and sounds an alert after too much screen time.

**Flip:**

- One fixed spot for keys, wallet, phone, and pills is a real help for anyone who loses things. The tray works without the sensors.
- "Did I already take my pill today?" is a real question. A pill box with a slot for each day answers it without tracking anything.
- Putting the phone in a physical place away from you cuts use better than an alert does.

## 2. GPS That Gives the Wrong Direction

**Bad idea:** Navigation that sends drivers to random places on purpose.

**Flip:**

- A random destination can be a feature: "take me somewhere new within a 15-minute walk" for people who want to explore a city.
- GPS already gives wrong directions indoors. The blue dot drifts, and the map doesn't know floors, hallways, or rooms. Directions that work inside buildings are the useful opposite. **(Led to top idea 1.)**

## 3. The 80-Pound Student Computer

**Bad idea:** A cheap student computer that weighs 80 pounds and has a poor battery.

**Flip:**

- Students don't have to carry the heavy part. A powerful machine can stay in a dorm or lab while the student carries a light screen and connects to it remotely.
- Affordable is the right goal. Cheap, sturdy computers that are meant to stay in one place could serve students who can't afford a laptop.
- Poor battery life points to a campus need: charging spots and lockers between classes.

## 4. Messenger You Cannot Mute

**Bad idea:** Every message rings at full volume, and you can't turn it down.

**Flip:**

- Some messages really shouldn't be missed: a call from an aging parent, or an on-call alert. A channel that only a few chosen people can use, and that always gets through Do Not Disturb, is useful.
- Because nothing can be muted, people would only use it for things that matter. Senders would have to think before they send.

## 5. Permanent Nuclear Alarm Clock

**Bad idea:** An alarm you can set once and never change or unplug.

**Flip:**

- Waking at the same time every day is good for sleep. In the brainstorm someone said "it's good for education," and that there were "two sides to that."
- A softer version is a commitment device: you can change the alarm, but the change only takes effect a week later, so you can't push it back the night before.
- An alarm that can't be unplugged by accident is reliable. A battery backup does the job without nuclear power.

## 6. Unstoppable Class-Punctuality Wearable

**Bad idea:** A wearable that tracks your location and vibrates until you head to class, with no way to turn it off.

**Flip:**

- Knowing the real travel time from where you are to the exact room is valuable. The person who pitched it said it "just ensures you to be punctual."
- The engine underneath it (TopoNavi) knows the inside of buildings: rooms, hallways, stairs, elevators, and which doors you can use. That is the useful part, without the wearable or the forced vibration. **(Led to top idea 1.)**
- A reminder you choose to turn on, with a strength you choose, keeps the push without the invasion.

## 7. Reverse Ozempic

**Bad idea:** A drug that makes people gain weight instead of lose it.

**Flip:**

- Some people do need help gaining or keeping weight: people with poor appetite from illness or its treatment, older adults, and athletes. That need gets far less attention than weight loss.
- A non-drug version is support for eating more: calorie-dense meal ideas and appetite tracking to share with a doctor or dietitian.
- Naming a product as the opposite of a famous one makes it instantly clear what it does.

## 8. Malicious Ad Blocker

**Bad idea:** An ad blocker that is secretly malware and steals your data.

**Flip:**

- Ad blockers need permission to read every page you visit, so people already trust them a lot. The real insight is how much trust people hand to browser extensions without checking.
- A tool that shows what each installed extension can access, and flags the risky ones, would help people use that trust carefully.

## 9. "Antidepressant" That Makes You Depressed

**Bad idea:** A drug sold as an antidepressant that makes you more depressed.

**Flip:**

- A new medication can affect mood in the first weeks, and people may not notice the change. A simple daily mood check-in that the patient chooses to share with their prescriber could catch problems early.
- The idea shows how much people rely on a label being honest. Clear, plain-language side-effect information is valuable on its own.

## 10. Salt That Makes Food Taste Worse

**Bad idea:** A seasoning that makes food taste worse.

**Flip:**

- Making something taste bad on purpose already works: bitter nail polish helps people stop biting their nails, and bitter additives keep children from swallowing household chemicals.
- A spray that makes a snack less appealing could help someone stop eating when they are already full.
- The useful opposite is salt that tastes like salt with less sodium, for people who have to cut back.

## 11. Grade-Shaming Accountability App

**Bad idea:** An app that sends every grade below an A to all your contacts, including employers.

**Flip:**

- Accountability does work better when someone else can see your progress. A study partner you choose, who sees only what you share, keeps the benefit and drops the shaming.
- An early warning to the student when a grade starts slipping, before it's too late, is the private and useful version.
- The line between help and harm is consent. Every share should be one the student chose.

## 12. Random Camera-Roll Snapchat

**Bad idea:** An app that sends random photos from your camera roll to random acquaintances.

**Flip:**

- An unexpected photo gives you a reason to message someone you haven't talked to in a while, which is often the hardest part of reconnecting.
- Old photos bring back people you have lost touch with. The camera roll is full of shared moments nobody looks at.
- Keep the surprise and drop the randomness: the app suggests a photo and people who were there, and you choose whether and to whom to send it. **(Led to top idea 2.)**
