# Interview Notes: Jude Darwish (NYC Housing)

**Date:** October 1, 2026  
**Interviewer:** Izum Adnan

Jude found a two-bedroom East Village apartment for under $6,000 through StreetEasy after a broker search did not work out. After moving in, she encountered pests, a broken air conditioner, and surprise fees.

## Interview snapshot

- **Who:** International MS Civil Engineering student at NYU (Brooklyn), family in Dubai, Syracuse undergrad. Shares an East Village two-bedroom with her sister.
- **Problem confirmed?:** Yes, strongly. She had one week in a hotel to find a place, the broker search failed, management denied a pest history that turned out to be real, and there were costs she hadn't been told about.
- **Key quote:** "I felt extremely scammed and like manipulated with that because that's like, it is the landlord's responsibility to make sure that the apartment is pest free."
- **Emotional intensity:** 8/10. "Very, very overwhelming"; "in the verge of tears" after the broker tours; "that's not fair" about the surprise late-August rent.
- **Current solution:** StreetEasy, an Excel tracker (link, address, laundry, guarantor acceptance, days on market), then a Google Doc, WhatsApp and video of every tour. Applied to two places at once.
- **Money spent?:** Yes. Paid a broker who found nothing suitable, hotel nights, $600 per person in unannounced late-August rent, and a replacement AC unit.
- **Biggest pain:** Deciding under time pressure, then hidden problems with the unit after signing.
- **Referral?:** None yet. Mentioned a friend moving to NYC in about a year; ask again.
- **Follow-up?:** Not discussed.
- **Surprise:** Their final choice came down to approval odds, not preference. They picked the unit with one competing applicant over the one with about 10.
- **Signal:** Green. Strong need for verified unit details, true monthly cost and help getting applications ready for international renters.

## Getting started

Jude came to NYC for an NYU master's after completing her undergraduate degree at Syracuse. She wanted to remain in New York, and NYU's reputation helped make the decision.

1. Browsed StreetEasy from Dubai to understand the market.
2. Made a shortlist about a week before arriving.
3. Arrived without housing and stayed in an expensive hotel, creating urgency.
4. Her dad suggested a broker; they toured several places over 4-5 days, but none fit.
5. Jude and her sister returned to StreetEasy, toured apartments from two management companies, and applied to both at once.
6. They signed with the apartment that approved them fastest and had fewer competing applicants.
7. Moved in during the last days of August, before classes began September 2.

Two hard constraints shaped the search: secure a lease before the semester and ensure laundry was in the unit or building.

## Search process

- The full search lasted about one week. They toured 2-3 places daily and found independent leads by browsing StreetEasy at night.
- Her dad's planned return flight added pressure to sign quickly.
- StreetEasy was the dominant source. Listers responded within 12 hours, and toured apartments matched their photos.
- The broker had good intentions but showed units that were too small or about $1,000 above budget.
- Jude trusted StreetEasy because listings matched reality; she felt management misled them about pests.

### Listings that did not match expectations

1. A $7,000 two-bedroom was large but had a slanted kitchen floor caused by a past laundry leak, no washer or dryer, and a long walk up stairs.
2. A higher-floor apartment had a window opening onto a neighbor's balcony, creating privacy concerns.
3. The chosen apartment had a denied pest history. Within a week, Jude found cockroaches and two mice. Four holes behind the oven needed patching, a process that took about a month; she still had not used the oven.

The landlord often took days to respond. The super responded within 24 hours and was helpful. Building staff patched holes for free, while mouse removal occurred within a day of a sighting.

## Tracking and decision criteria

- Toured about 10 places: seven with the broker over 4-5 days, then three independently.
- While in Dubai, used an Excel tracker with StreetEasy link, address, laundry status, guarantor acceptance, and days on market.
- In NYC, used a Google Doc and WhatsApp. Her sister recorded every tour.
- The search felt very overwhelming because apartments blended together, hotel costs accrued, school approached, and her dad's flight loomed.
- Priority order: laundry, avoiding a ground-floor apartment, then price.
- Applied to two places simultaneously. One had approximately 10 applicants; the other had one, so they accepted the faster approval.
- Checked whether rent was negotiable and used each unit's flaws in negotiation.

## Missing listing information and neighborhood choice

- Wanted clear information on whether AC was included, whether it was a window or through-the-wall unit, and whether it worked. Her bedroom's through-the-wall unit was broken and needed replacement.
- Compared neighborhoods by commute. East Village offered nearby Trader Joe's, Target, the NYU gym, restaurants, cafes, and a safe, busy feel, but had many undergraduates and a 30-minute subway commute for Jude.
- Downtown Brooklyn had a similar price and newer high-rises, but less to do than Manhattan.
- Williamsburg had decent prices and young professionals, but a less direct commute to Downtown Brooklyn.
- With similar prices, the sisters chose Manhattan, leaving each with an approximately 30-minute commute.

## Competition and application

- Lost a third-floor unit with an elevator and doorman because it had about 10 applications and someone ready to sign.
- For the current unit, her dad built rapport with the agent and they said they could complete everything by the next day.
- Jude felt stuck after broker tours: small, expensive units were being taken while they searched. The process clicked when the sisters found and toured places on StreetEasy independently.
- Signing took about two days: assemble money, gather documents, await approval, then sign at the leasing office.
- Documents: passport and ID details, visa status and travel history, credit-card history, guarantor details and income, and proof of purpose in NYC such as an acceptance letter or job offer.
- The main international-student hurdle was a third-party guarantor. Not every building accepts one, so it was a tracker field. US credit and an SSN did not come up.

## Reflections

- Hardest part: finding something affordable that still met must-haves.
- Advice: skip the broker; search StreetEasy directly; do not rush applications; avoid first-floor units; look for rent-stabilized apartments; check price history and annual rent increases; apply to multiple places; organize documents before touring.
- Moving support and AC-installation assistance could help. She mentioned Piece of Cake for local moving help.
