# Interview Notes: Sean (NYC Housing)

**Interviewer:** Jorim Chua  
**Source:** Recorded transcript, "Housing Interview - Shan" (team Google Drive)

Sean is an international junior from Singapore who has rented three times off campus: a Facebook sublet on 125th Street, an interim apartment found with a real estate agent, and his current 3-bedroom on 108th Street found on StreetEasy. Broker fees, unclear guarantor rules and a general distrust of landlords shaped every search.

## Interview snapshot

- **Who:** Columbia junior studying political science and economics, from Singapore. Started school in the spring, so on-campus housing was nearly impossible. Now shares a 3-bedroom on 108th Street with two other international students.
- **Problem confirmed?:** Yes. Without being prompted, he described the process as opaque, said broker fees were unclear and paid twice, and said listings don't show whether third-party guarantors are accepted.
- **Key quote:** "There's always that sense of like distrust which makes things a bit more like transactional."
- **Emotional intensity:** 7/10. He used strong language about paying broker fees on both sides and about the 80x guarantor rule, and said he always feels like he's "getting scammed" by service providers.
- **Current solution:** Facebook sublet groups for his first place. A real estate agent for his second, which he regretted. StreetEasy plus a friend who toured in person and sent videos for his current place. A third-party guarantor service, which costs about one month's rent.
- **Money spent?:** Yes. He paid broker fees on both sides (his agent's and the landlord's agent's) and about one month's rent for a third-party guarantor. The upfront fees made him stay an extra year so he wouldn't lose what he'd paid.
- **Biggest pain:** Fees and requirements he couldn't see ahead of time (broker fees, guarantor acceptance), plus trying to judge a place from Singapore.
- **Referral?:** Not asked.
- **Follow-up?:** Not discussed.
- **Surprise:** The fees changed his behavior and not just his budget: "because we paid so much like upfront, it kind of didn't make sense to switch so quickly." He also wanted an independent advisor, not a better listing site: "somebody to consult that wasn't just trying to charge me money."
- **Signal:** Green. A strong, specific need for listing transparency (broker fee, guarantor acceptance, neighborhood) and for an unconflicted guide, especially for international renters.

## Background

- Junior at Columbia studying political science and economics, from Singapore.
- Moved to New York for college; drawn to the city for the arts, Broadway, jazz and finance.
- Has moved multiple times: one sublet, one rental found with a real estate agent, and his current StreetEasy rental.
- Off campus because the housing waitlist prioritizes distance from your permanent address. Once his address switched from Singapore to a local one, he kept falling down the list.

## First move: Facebook sublet (spring semester)

- Starting in the spring made on-campus housing nearly impossible, because few students leave mid-year.
- A friend's sister, a recent Columbia graduate, added him to Facebook sublet groups.
- Found a couple moving to D.C. for a White House externship and negotiated a one-semester sublet of a one-bedroom on 125th Street.
- Spent about 1–2 hours a day browsing and reached out to about 10 listings. Response and negotiation rates were good.
- His sister, a Columbia Law graduate, advised staying close to campus or the subway. In hindsight it was good advice, since 125th turned out to be farther than ideal.

## Second move: real estate agent

- The agent still relied on StreetEasy and didn't find listings himself; he mainly handled tours and paperwork.
- They paid broker fees on both sides, which felt unnecessary.
- They decided to cut out the middleman next time.

## Current move: StreetEasy (108th Street)

- 3-bedroom shared with two other international students, found on StreetEasy.
- A friend had lived across the hall in the same building, which made them feel more confident.
- Sean wasn't in New York, so a friend toured in person and sent videos.
- Requirements: 2 bathrooms and a washer/dryer in the unit. Sean pays more for the master bedroom with its own bathroom. The trade-off was having no real living room.
- Looked at only about 5 places because they settled on the familiar building quickly.

## Tracking and listings

- No formal tracker. Facebook Messenger worked as a log because he only used it for housing at the time.
- Saw "catfishing" listings whose photos didn't match reality, but no outright scams.

## Pain points

- **DIY market:** In Singapore people usually hire an agent; NYC expects renters to do it themselves.
- **Not being in person:** Hard to verify spaces from abroad. Furniture he ordered based on floor plans didn't fit.
- **Broker fee opacity:** Hard to know whether a fee applies, how much it is and who pays it.
- **Guarantor requirements:** Guarantors must earn 80x the monthly rent, which is nearly impossible for a 3-bedroom. Pooling guarantors doesn't work when everyone is international.
- **Third-party guarantors:** A necessary workaround that costs about one month's rent, but listings don't say whether they're accepted.
- **Fees affect how long people stay:** High upfront fees made him stay longer than planned.
- **Distrust:** Feels landlords always know more than tenants and are inclined to overcharge.
- **Time pressure:** Landlords push "sign today or lose it."
- **Neighborhoods:** Hard to judge from a listing or map. Things like nearby Asian food only became clear after living there.

## Reflections

- Would expand the search to the Upper West Side (70s–90s). There's likely less competition from Columbia students, and the commute is only a few stops longer.
- Wants a trusted, knowledgeable advisor with no fee-driven conflict of interest.
- Wants listings to show broker fees, whether third-party guarantors are accepted, and what the neighborhood is like.
