# Startup Studio: AI-Accelerated Building & Validation

Course ID: COMS 4995, section 014 
Semester: Fall 2026 
Instructor: Ken St. Clair

## Course Summary

Startup Studio is a studio-style course in which students form teams and build a startup across the course of the semester. Teams will launch real products and acquire real users, learning along the way how to build products that people want, need, and love.

The course has three main pillars:

- **Agentic engineering:** using AI tools to rapidly build software that is reliable and secure.
- **UX techniques:** methods for advancing product-market fit, including user interviews and usability testing.
- **Startup knowledge:** growth hacking, presentation skills, fundraising, and how founders operate.

By the end of the semester, students will have built and validated a technology product and gained experience in both technical development and entrepreneurial growth methods.

## Learning Objectives

Within the semester, students will:

- **Collaborate effectively** within teams, and learn how to provide effective feedback to other teams.
- **Apply AI tools** to accelerate software development and product design.
- **Conduct UX research**, including user interviews and usability testing, to uncover real user pain points and problems.
- **Design and execute demand validation experiments**, including paid ad campaigns.
- **Apply product management thinking**, translating ideas into roadmaps, selecting features for MVPs, and prioritizing based on feedback and data analysis.
- **Achieve certifications** that demonstrate proficiency in digital marketing and user analytics.
- **Present products and features**, and tell the story behind them, including for fundraising.

## Grading Rubric Overview

### Demo Day (30%)

**Audience:** Class peers, instructors, invited alumni, industry professionals, potential mentors.
**Format:**
- Each team gets 15 minutes to present and answer questions.
- Pitches will be VC-style pitches
- Optional: Post-demo networking session with audience members.

| Criteria | Excellent (A) | Good (B) | Satisfactory (C) | Poor (D/F) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product Development | Functional, innovative, addresses validated user need | Functional, moderate innovation, partial validation | Basic functionality, minimal validation | Incomplete or non-functional |
| Validation Efforts | Clear evidence of demand validation + user feedback integration | Some validation, some user research | Limited validation or user research | No meaningful validation |
| Presentation | Clear, persuasive, well-structured pitch | Adequate presentation, minor gaps | Disorganized or unclear | Unclear or missing |
| Iteration | Demonstrated learning + multiple iterations | Some iteration | Minimal iteration | No iteration |

### Milestone Submissions (30%)

| Criteria | On Time, Quality, Iterative Progress |
|---|---|
| Full credit | On time, high quality, shows learning |
| Partial credit | Late, incomplete, or poor quality |

### Peer Reviews (20%)

Students rate each of their teammates on 5 criteria, scored 1–4 from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree:

- Contributes meaningfully to group discussions.
- Completes group assignments on time.
- Prepares work in a quality manner.
- Demonstrates a cooperative and supportive attitude.
- Contributes significantly to the success of the project.

This exists to encourage equal work and contribution within teams, not to produce a public ranking.

### User Growth (10%)

Acquiring real users is part of the grade. Points scale linearly with real users acquired: 1 point per 10 users, up to a cap of 10 points at 100 or more users. A team with 45 users earns 4 points; a team with 100 or more earns full credit.

## Intellectual Property & Public Disclosure Policy

Key points:

- **No Confidentiality:** All work developed in this class is open and may be shared publicly.
- **Pre-existing IP:** Students retain rights to intellectual property they bring into the course.
- **Continuation Beyond Class:** If teams wish to pursue their project commercially post-class, they should negotiate ownership and IP rights internally and/or with external advisors.
- **Site Shutdown or Continuation:** Teams will decide post-semester whether to shut down their products/sites or continue development independently.

Note: This course is not an incubator, and Columbia University does not claim ownership of student-developed IP.

## Required Materials

- **A frontier AI coding model subscription,** at a rate that supports real software development work.
- **A domain name,** typically under $30/year through a registrar like Cloudflare.
- **Hosting,** for example Railway at around $5/month.
- **A team ad-spend budget of $100 ($25/person)** for running paid ad campaigns. This is not funded by the school. Teams cover it themselves.
- **Optional: user incentives for interviews.** Compensating interview participants isn't required, but teams that choose to should plan for up to another $100.

Additional AI tools beyond a frontier coding model may be purchased at each student's discretion. Most of what the course practices can be done without any other paid tools, as long as you have a good frontier model with an adequate token allowance.

## Additional Notes

- **Consumer-focused, not B2B.** Due to time constraints, products should target consumers, not businesses. B2B products carry longer sales cycles than can fit inside a single semester.
- **Off-limits categories.** Products may not include medical diagnosis or treatment, financial advice, anything involving minors' data, or legal advice.
- **Web-based products are preferred.** Students who want to build a native iOS app instead will need to purchase an Apple Developer account ($99/year) and get approved for the App Store (up to 2 weeks lead time). If students build an app they are required to launch it on the App Store.
- **Guest Speakers (tentative):** Sessions with product designers, UX researchers, and growth marketers may be scheduled to provide real-world perspectives.
